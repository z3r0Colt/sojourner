# Sojourner 0.5.0

**A free, private, offline Bible study companion for Windows and Linux.**
_Don't forget the Library Packs!_

Sojourner puts Scripture beside the historic commentaries, confessions and reference works of the church, with the Hebrew and Greek underneath and the tools to prepare a sermon on top. It needs no account and no internet connection. Your notes, highlights, prayers and sermons stay in one file on your own computer.

0.5.0 is the release for someone opening Sojourner for the first time. A new install is asked one question, **What brings you here?**, and is shown the pages for that and no more. The tour is now three things to do in the Bible itself. Every icon tells you its name the moment the pointer rests on it. If you already use Sojourner, nothing you rely on has moved out of reach: you are not asked the question, and you keep every page.

## New in 0.5.0

### A first hour for someone new

- **One question at the door.** A new install asks **What brings you here?** Reading the Bible, studying it deeply, preparing to preach or teach, leading family worship, or "show me everything". One click answers it. The answer chooses which pages the sidebar shows, where the app opens, and the arrangement it starts in. Someone who came to read sees seven pages where there were twenty-one.
- **Nothing is taken away.** The pages not shown wait under **More** in the sidebar, in the same order as always. **Go to** (Ctrl+K) and **Search** reach every page whatever the sidebar shows.
- **Change your mind any time.** Settings → Reading → **I use Sojourner for** changes the answer, and **Sidebar pages** beneath it turns pages on and off one by one.
- **A tour of three things to do.** The old tour pointed at ten parts of the window and was passed with ten presses of Next. The new one asks you to do the three things the whole app turns on: pick a verse, open a commentary on it, and write a note. It waits for each, never takes over the keyboard, stands aside while you write, and can be skipped a step at a time. It runs on a phone as well.
- **Help has a door of its own.** **Help**, at the foot of the sidebar, opens one page with the tour, the keyboard shortcuts and the tutorial. (It was Settings → Tutorial.)

### If you already use Sojourner

- **You are not asked the question,** you keep every page in the sidebar, and your panes, workspaces and settings are as you left them.
- **Two names have changed.** The **Resources** page is now called **Books**, since that is what it holds. The Lexicon, Dictionary, Encyclopedia and Factbook sit together in the sidebar under a new heading, **Look up**.
- **Help** is a new row at the foot of the sidebar.

### Easier to find your way

- **Every icon tells you its name.** Rest the pointer on any icon and its name appears in a third of a second, then at once on the next one along, so a toolbar can be read by drawing the pointer across it. The name also shows when you reach an icon with the Tab key, which it never did before.
- **The study panes have names.** The strip of icons beside the Bible opens to show each pane's name (Commentary, Cross references, Encyclopedia, People and places and the rest) under **Study this passage**. A new install starts with the names showing. The button at the foot of the strip shows or hides them.
- **Translations are named in their menu.** The translation menu in the Bible's toolbar gives each translation's full name beside its code ("DBY  Darby Bible", "WBS  Webster's Bible"), under Historic English, Modern English, and Greek, Hebrew and Latin.
- **Pages open where there is room.** With a commentary beside the text and last clicked, a page chosen from the sidebar (Settings, Books, Guided study) used to open squeezed into the commentary's narrow column. It now opens in the widest pane.
- **On a large monitor the Bible's toolbar names its tools** (Find, Notes, Read aloud, Bookmarks, Text, View, Focus).
- **A verse's number sits at its first line** on a wide screen. It used to sit halfway down a long verse.

### Less in the way

- **The sermon tools are for those writing sermons.** "Send to sermon" and "Save as illustration" sat on every verse, commentary entry, confession section and article. They now show if you came to preach or teach, if you chose everything, if you were using Sojourner before this release, or whenever a sermon is open. To have them always, choose **Preparing to preach or teach** or **Everything** in Settings → Reading.
- **A commentary entry's tools appear when the pointer is on it,** rather than standing in a column down the page.
- **Search shows the tabs that suit you.** Thirteen tabs wrapped onto three rows. A reader now sees five, and a tab with something found in it always appears.
- **The opening screen stays three seconds the first time,** and about one second after that.

### Guided study

- **The Guided study page shows its courses.** There is one, the Westminster Shorter Catechism, with how many of its lessons you have finished, and a place for the courses still being written. The page opens straight on the course, as before.

### Fixed

- **On a phone, "Commentary on this verse" could open several entries past the verse chosen** (Genesis 1:3 opened on verse 9). It now lands on the verse. This affects a phone using **Other devices**, and the web app.
- **The backup reminder could be skipped for a whole session** when the automatic-backup setting happened to be the last thing to load.

### Sojourner in your browser

The web app at **[app.gentleking.org](https://app.gentleking.org)** has everything above, and is already up to date.

- **The web app has analytics, and says so.** It uses Cloudflare Web Analytics to count visits and see which devices and browsers people use, so we know what to fix first. It sets no cookies and does not track you across sites. Settings → About says the same. **The Windows and Linux apps have no analytics at all,** as before.

### Behind the scenes

- The tests, the type checks and a linter now run on every change before it can reach you. One of the tests opens the whole Bible pane and tries it, which nothing did before.
- The project now keeps a changelog (`CHANGELOG.md`).

## What's inside

### Bibles
- **Thirteen English translations.**
  - Historic: King James (1769), American Standard (1901), Young's Literal Translation, Darby, Webster's Bible, Geneva (1599), Douay-Rheims and Tyndale.
  - Modern and free to share: the World English Bible, Berean Standard Bible, Literal Standard Version, and unfoldingWord's Literal and Simplified Texts.
- **The original languages.**
  - The Hebrew Old Testament (Leningrad Codex), read right to left, in a font that sets the vowel points where they belong.
  - Five editions of the Greek New Testament: Textus Receptus, Byzantine, SBL Greek New Testament, Westcott–Hort and Tregelles.
  - The Septuagint and the Clementine Vulgate.
  - All follow English chapter and verse numbering, so they line up with the English beside them. The Greek and Hebrew are shown exactly as their sources give them.
- **Your own translations and commentaries.** Add a translation you own from a Zefania XML file, or a commentary in ThML. They appear under **User provided**, and about fifty copyrighted modern translations are recognised and marked **Licensed**.

### Commentaries and confessions
- **Commentaries:**
  - Matthew Henry's whole *Commentary on the Whole Bible*, each paragraph under its verse.
  - Calvin's Commentaries, with the Calvin Translation Society's footnotes set apart as *Editor's notes*.
  - Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, Barnes' Notes on the New Testament, and Spurgeon's *Treasury of David* on every Psalm.
- **The Westminster Standards:** the Confession and the Larger and Shorter Catechisms with their Scripture proofs, a topic index, the parallels between them, and eleven commentaries: Hodge, Shaw, Vincent, Fisher and Erskine, Henry, Flavel, Whyte, Ridgley, and Beattie on all three.
- **The creeds:** the Apostles', Nicene and Athanasian creeds, and the Chalcedonian definition.
- **The Three Forms of Unity:** the Heidelberg Catechism, the Belgic Confession and the Canons of Dort with the Rejection of Errors.
- **For children:** the *Catechism for Young Children* (1840).

### Original-language study
- **Lexicons:** Strong's and Thayer's; the full Brown–Driver–Briggs, Abbott-Smith and Liddell–Scott–Jones; STEPBible's brief lexicons.
- **Interlinear, laid out like Blue Letter Bible's,** one row for each English phrase, with Strong's number, the original word, its root, transliteration and parsing.
- **Parsing in plain English,** with every term explained on the word's card.
- **Word study:** how often a word occurs and where, how the KJV renders it, its forms, related words, and every occurrence.
- **Search by grammar,** and **where the Greek editions differ** for any verse.

### Reference
- **Factbook:** 4,275 people, places and things, each Zechariah told apart by the verses that name him.
- **Timeline:** 464 biblical events from Creation to Paul in Rome, and 296 events of church history to the present, each dated from a source it quotes.
- **Atlas:** 1,285 places and 34 journeys on a real, offline map, with the Roman roads and the great Old Testament routes, a ruler for distances and travel times, and optional terrain and satellite imagery packs.
- **Encyclopedia and dictionaries:** the *International Standard Bible Encyclopedia* (9,349 articles), Easton's and Smith's combined, and Webster's 1828 dictionary for the older senses of the King James words.
- **Cross references:** over 340,000. Also two harmonies of the Gospels and the reading plans.

### The Psalter
- **The 1650 Scottish Metrical Psalter** on a page of its own, every setting singable with its tune played and the music shown.

## What you can do with it

**Read and study.** Split the window into up to eight panes, stack them as tabs, and save named workspaces. Linked panes follow each other: click a verse, and the commentary, cross references, confession proofs, encyclopedia, atlas, people and places, timeline and the books that cite it all turn to that verse. Compare translations, highlight in five colours, bookmark, and write notes on a verse, a highlight or a chapter.

**See who and what is in a passage.** **Color text** gives every person, place, time and number its own color, in every translation and in the Greek and Hebrew; click a word for every verse where it appears. **Who's speaking** sets God's own words in small capitals and Scripture quoted in a speech in italics. Search them with `tag:son`, `tag:places` or `speaker:god`.

**Search everything.** One box covers Scripture, commentaries, lexicons, the Factbook, confessions, the encyclopedia, your books, notes, prayers, sermons and illustrations, with exact phrases, OR, exclusions, `in:psalms`, `t:kjv`, Strong's numbers and older spellings.

**Prepare and preach.** A sermon editor whose points are its outline, with live Scripture blocks and sources sent from anywhere in the app. Typed blocks, templates, sermon ideas, a preparation track, timed rehearsals, an illustrations file, a full-screen preaching mode, a podium file for a phone or tablet, slides with PowerPoint export, fill-in handouts, and series that become reading plans for the congregation.

**Learn.** A guided study of the Westminster Shorter Catechism: 45 lessons covering all 107 questions, taught from Watson, Henry and the other old expositors, each lesson a short walk through the app's own tools. Go deeper with the Larger Catechism and the Confession, print your notebook, or print handouts and a leader's copy for a class. Family worship links the week's question to its lesson.

**Pray and remember.** A Today page, reading plans, a prayer journal and prayer list, and Scripture and catechism memory with spaced repetition. A calendar of the days you practised, not a streak.

**Family worship.** A reading, a psalm to sing, a catechism question, a verse to learn and people to pray for, moving on when you gather. **Gather round** fills the screen in large type, and with **Other devices** it can run on the television or a tablet at the table.

**Listen.** An offline neural voice reads any chapter, commentary, book or memory card aloud, starting where you are.

**Build a library.** Install any of the four book shelves (see the Library Packs release). Beside each verse, a count shows how many of your books cite it. Add your own EPUB, PDF and MOBI books and audio and video files on the **Books** page.

**Keep your data safe.** Automatic backups, export and import, an integrity check, a copy to a OneDrive, Dropbox or other synced folder, and a Trash that keeps deleted things for thirty days.

**Make it comfortable.** Seven themes, a dyslexia-friendly font, adjustable text and reduced motion. A built-in tutorial covers every feature (**Help**, at the foot of the sidebar), and Ctrl+/ lists the keyboard shortcuts.

## Private by design

The Windows and Linux apps have no account, no analytics, and send nothing anywhere. They go online only when you press **Check for updates** in Settings → About. **Other devices**, when you turn it on, serves the app to devices on your own network and nowhere else. Library and map packs are installed from files you download yourself.

The web app is a separate thing. It keeps your notes in your own browser and has no account, but it reads its books from our server as you open them, and it counts visits with Cloudflare Web Analytics, as described above.

## Known limitations

- **Windows and Linux** on the computer; no Mac yet. Phones, tablets and Macs can use the web app, or **Other devices** while the computer is on.
- **Linux needs a recent system:** Ubuntu 24.04, Linux Mint 22, Debian 13, Fedora 40 or newer.
- **No copyrighted modern resources are included.** A translation you own can be added from a Zefania XML file.
- **The Windows installer isn't code-signed yet** (see "First run on Windows" above).
- **No automatic updates.** Check for updates in Settings → About, then download the new installer or package.
- **The Bible toolbar's tools are icons without written names** unless the pane is very wide. Rest the pointer on one for its name.
